DBA

Dynamic block activation (DBA) divides a simulated field into GPU-friendly blocks and activates them where more computation is needed, such as near a moving solid–liquid interface. Introduced by Ruoyao Zhang and Yang Xia, it provides the starting point for sp-DBA.

sp-DBA

I led the design and implementation of sp-DBA with my advisor, Prof. Yang Xia, to reduce redundant Fourier-domain computation. Its shared core maps spectral blocks to CUDA thread blocks for full, smoothed, or skipped updates. Boundary smoothing and periodic refresh manage activation transitions, while the equations, numerical scheme, and FFT/IFFT workflow remain unchanged.

Applications span phase-field crystal (PFC) models of evolving crystal grains, reaction–cross-diffusion (RCD) models of biological pattern formation, and digital backpropagation (DBP) for optical-signal recovery. With eigendecomposition, sp-DBA achieved up to 28.1× speedup in RCD updates over direct-matrix Regular. Controlled Gaussian-wave-packet DBP tests achieved 8.4× overall speedup over full-update Regular.

Phase-field crystal (PFC)

Materials science · Polycrystalline coarsening

Phase-field crystal (PFC) models describe a crystal as a continuous density field whose peaks mark atomic-scale lattice order. They let us follow defects and grain boundaries over long evolution times. In the structural PFC (XPFC) example below, most Fourier amplitude lies near narrow rings, so sp-DBA concentrates updates there as grains grow and their boundaries move.

Blocks switch between active, transition, and frozen states. Frozen blocks retain their values; the numerical representation and full FFT/IFFT workflow stay in place.

Reaction–cross-diffusion (RCD)

Biological pattern formation

Reaction–cross-diffusion (RCD) models describe how interacting components react and spread to form patterns. A component’s motion can depend on how the others vary across space. In this eight-component test, sp-DBA concentrates coupled updates in the Fourier bands where patterns develop.

Digital backpropagation (DBP)

Optical communications

Digital backpropagation (DBP) numerically reverses optical-signal propagation to compensate for distortion. In our controlled Gaussian-wave-packet tests, most signal power stays within narrow frequency bands. sp-DBA targets those bands during linear updates; nonlinear steps and FFT/IFFT operations remain unchanged.

Jingkun Jiang, Pingchuan Deng, and Yang Xia. sp-DBA: a general framework for adaptive transform-domain computation. Preprint (2026).

Distributed FFTs

Larger simulations need to share a field across GPUs. I implemented distributed fast Fourier transforms (FFTs) for PFC using slab decomposition, local cuFFT transforms, and MPI redistribution. Block-state exchange coordinates adaptive updates at rank boundaries; the complete FFT/IFFT workflow remains in place.

Computational Materials

Phase-field crystal models

I developed PFC, structural PFC (XPFC), and amplitude PFC (APFC) solvers for crystalline microstructure evolution. PFC/XPFC use Fourier pseudo-spectral discretization with fourth-order exponential time differencing (ETD4), implemented on CPUs and GPUs.

I extract density peaks, calculate local orientations from sixfold bond order, and export particle data with in-plane quaternions for inspection in OVITO. My segmentation code uses periodic neighbors and region growing to measure grain areas, equivalent diameters, and size distributions.

Amplitude phase-field crystal (APFC)

I implemented two-dimensional APFC solvers with three complex amplitudes and reconstructed density fields, including CPU/FFTW and CUDA/cuFFT versions.

Phase-field (PF) modeling

Three-dimensional dendritic growth

Phase-field models describe a growing solid with a continuous field, so its moving boundary emerges from the equations. I implemented a three-dimensional solver coupling crystal phase and temperature, using finite differences with CPU/OpenMP, MPI, and CUDA versions. The image comes from early development.
