DBA

Prior work

Dynamic Block Activation (DBA), developed by Ruoyao Zhang and Yang Xia, is a prior real-space adaptive acceleration framework that selectively updates GPU-aligned data blocks across materials science, biophysics, and fluid dynamics. In the reported benchmarks, it accelerates computation with minor solver changes while retaining numerical accuracy, with near-ideal strong and weak scaling across multiple GPUs. sp-DBA extends this block-activation foundation to transform-domain workflows. Scientific Computing & HPC

sp-DBA

Transform-domain workflows repeatedly update large spectral or frequency-space arrays even when significant changes concentrate in localized modes, while widespread real-space variation limits traditional spatial block adaptivity. To address this, we developed sp-DBA, an adaptive block-activation framework with GPU-aligned execution.

I led the design and implementation under Prof. Yang Xia’s supervision.

Details arXiv

Jingkun Jiang, Pingchuan Deng, and Yang Xia*. sp-DBA: a general framework for adaptive transform-domain computation. (2026).

Key Advantages

  1. Efficient GPU execution. Hardware-aligned block adaptivity reduces unnecessary updates and thread divergence.

  2. Compatibility with existing optimizations. Works alongside optimized transforms and model-specific acceleration.

  3. Solver compatibility. Preserves equations, discretization, and transformed representations.

  4. Lightweight integration. Reuses existing update routines with minimal solver-specific code changes.

Polycrystalline Coarsening (Phase-field crystal, PFC)

PFC models describe crystal microstructure through a spatially periodic density field. In our coarsening tests, the dominant Fourier amplitudes concentrate near Bragg rings (ring-shaped features associated with the crystal lattice), providing localized spectral blocks for sp-DBA to update selectively. Pattern Formation (Reaction–cross-diffusion, RCD)

RCD models describe interacting species and coupled transport. More components make Fourier-space updates increasingly costly, giving selective execution greater value for complex pattern-forming systems. I combined sp-DBA with eigendecomposition, retaining close agreement with full-update morphologies and structure factors in the tested cases. More details →

Optical Communications (Digital backpropagation, DBP)

DBP is an engineering transform-domain workflow for recovering signals propagated through multimode optical fibers. I integrated sp-DBA into the repeated frequency-domain linear updates of a split-step solver. Controlled Gaussian wave-packet tests retained close agreement with the transmitted reference; in the reported benchmark, the frequency-domain linear updates were 21.3× faster than full-update Regular. More details →

I am also exploring how these ideas might extend to other transforms, multiresolution methods, and unstructured meshes.

Computational Modeling

Dendritic Growth (Phase-field model, PF)

I implemented a three-dimensional phase-field solver coupling crystal growth with heat diffusion, using finite differences and CPU/GPU parallel versions. By varying anisotropy, undercooling, temperature sensitivity, and seed size, I examined how these choices affect branching and tip shape. More details →

Microstructure Evolution

Structural phase-field crystal (XPFC) uses correlation kernels C2 to select lattice spacings and symmetries. I implemented Fourier-space solvers to compare how different kernels shape two-dimensional density patterns and spectra. More details →

Amplitude phase-field crystal (APFC) describes crystal order through slowly varying complex amplitudes Aj and reconstructs the density ψ. I implemented CPU and GPU Fourier-space solvers and inspected grain contact through reconstructed density, amplitude, and phase fields. More details →

Grain and Defect Analysis

I combine long-time phase-field crystal (PFC) simulations with a single-layer grain-analysis pipeline to follow slow microstructural changes while retaining lattice-scale detail. C++/CUDA extracts density-peak particles and crystal orientations, Python reconstructs grains and calculates grain-size statistics, and OVITO supports inspection and visualization. This connects visible defect evolution with quantitative grain measurements. The video illustrates a small-angle grain rotating and shrinking as its dislocation ring moves inward. More details →

Reactive Phase Separation (Reactive Model B)
