DBA

Dynamic block activation (DBA), developed by Ruoyao Zhang and Yang Xia, activates computation near evolving features such as solid–liquid interfaces and reduces work in quieter regions. Its software–hardware co-design maps numerical blocks to GPU thread blocks and stores activation parameters in shared memory, keeping threads on a common execution path. By overlapping boundary communication with computation, the reported dendritic-growth tests achieved near-ideal strong and weak scaling on up to 32 GPUs.

sp-DBA

DBA brings adaptivity to real-space simulations. Extending it to Fourier space is less direct: each mode spans the whole physical domain, so local activation rules cannot simply be reused. I led the design and implementation of sp-DBA with my advisor, Prof. Yang Xia, to make transform-domain workflows adaptive while retaining established solvers.

The framework matches GPU execution, complements optimized transforms, preserves existing numerical methods, and is simple to integrate. We demonstrated it in phase-field crystal (PFC) models of crystalline materials, reaction–cross-diffusion (RCD) models of biological pattern formation, and digital backpropagation (DBP) for optical communications.

With eigendecomposition, sp-DBA achieved up to 28.1× faster RCD updates than direct-matrix Regular. Controlled Gaussian-wave-packet DBP tests reached 8.4× overall acceleration over full-update Regular.

For periodic problems, pseudo-spectral solvers use fast Fourier transforms (FFTs) to move between real space and Fourier space. Nonlinear terms are evaluated in real space; spatial derivatives become multiplications in Fourier space. sp-DBA adapts the transformed-field updates between transforms.

Phase-field crystal (PFC)

Materials science · Polycrystalline coarsening

PFC models describe a crystal through a continuous density field whose peaks resolve atomic-scale lattice order. High-order spatial terms and periodic lattice order make Fourier pseudo-spectral methods a natural choice. In the structural PFC (XPFC) example below, strong Fourier amplitudes concentrate in narrow rings associated with the lattice spacing. sp-DBA focuses updates around these bands as grains and their boundaries evolve.

Reaction–cross-diffusion (RCD)

Biological pattern formation

Reaction–cross-diffusion (RCD) models describe how interacting components react and spread to form patterns. A component’s motion can depend on how the others vary across space. In this eight-component test, sp-DBA concentrates coupled updates in the Fourier bands where patterns develop.

Digital backpropagation (DBP)

Optical communications

Digital backpropagation (DBP) numerically reverses optical-signal propagation to compensate for distortion. In our controlled Gaussian-wave-packet tests, most signal power stays within narrow frequency bands. sp-DBA targets those bands during linear updates; nonlinear steps and FFT/IFFT operations remain unchanged.

Submission history

Distributed FFTs

I implemented distributed FFTs for PFC by splitting the simulation field into slabs across GPUs. Each GPU performs local transforms; MPI redistributes data between stages. Neighboring ranks exchange block-state metadata to coordinate adaptive updates at slab boundaries. The complete FFT/IFFT workflow and global redistribution remain in place.

Computational Materials

Phase-field crystal models

I developed PFC, structural PFC (XPFC), and amplitude PFC (APFC) solvers for crystalline microstructure evolution. PFC/XPFC use Fourier pseudo-spectral discretization with fourth-order exponential time differencing (ETD4), implemented on CPUs and GPUs.

I extract density peaks, calculate local orientations from sixfold bond order, and export particle data with in-plane quaternions for inspection in OVITO. My segmentation code uses periodic neighbors and region growing to measure grain areas, equivalent diameters, and size distributions.

Amplitude phase-field crystal (APFC)

I implemented two-dimensional APFC solvers with three complex amplitudes and reconstructed density fields, including CPU/FFTW and CUDA/cuFFT versions.

Phase-field (PF) modeling

Three-dimensional dendritic growth

Phase-field models describe a growing solid with a continuous field, so its moving boundary emerges from the equations. I implemented a three-dimensional solver coupling crystal phase and temperature, using finite differences with CPU/OpenMP, MPI, and CUDA versions. The image comes from early development.
