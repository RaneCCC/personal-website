DBA · Prior work

Phase-field models describe evolving structures through continuous order parameters. In crystal growth, most changes occur near moving interfaces, while a uniform-grid solver updates the entire domain. Adaptive mesh refinement (AMR) reduces this imbalance, but changing grids and irregular data access complicate efficient GPU execution. Developed by Ruoyao Zhang and Yang Xia, dynamic block activation (DBA) instead adapts work on a fixed grid. GPU-aligned blocks, shared-memory states, and overlapping communication with computation make the method suited to modern parallel hardware. The framework covers materials, biophysics, and fluid dynamics, with near-ideal scaling to 32 GPUs demonstrated for dendritic growth.

Ruoyao Zhang and Yang Xia. A dynamic block activation framework for continuum models. Nature Computational Science 5, 345–354 (2025).

sp-DBA

DBA is effective when useful computation is localized in real space. Transform-domain solvers pose a different problem: in Fourier space, each coefficient describes a basis function spanning the domain, so a block is no longer a local patch of material. I led the design and implementation of sp-DBA with Prof. Yang Xia to bring adaptive acceleration to these workflows. It coordinates block activation, transition smoothing, and consistency controls while keeping the equations and numerical methods intact.

By skipping unnecessary updates and keeping each GPU block on a common execution path, sp-DBA reduces work and limits thread divergence. It complements optimized transforms, including FFTW, cuFFT, and distributed FFT libraries. Its modular design reuses existing update kernels; direct integration added 8–16% extra solver-core code in our three examples, excluding adapters and the shared framework. We demonstrated it in phase-field crystal (PFC) simulations of materials, reaction–cross-diffusion (RCD) for biological patterns, and digital backpropagation (DBP) for optical communications.

Jingkun Jiang, Pingchuan Deng, and Yang Xia. sp-DBA: a general framework for adaptive transform-domain computation. Preprint (2026).

A phase field tracks the growing crystal while a temperature field carries the released latent heat. I implemented Kobayashi’s three-dimensional model with finite differences, then built OpenMP, MPI, and CUDA versions. Varying its directional driving term lets me inspect how anisotropy shapes branching.
