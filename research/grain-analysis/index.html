From density fields to grain statistics

I built a pipeline that converts single-layer phase-field crystal (PFC) density fields into crystal orientations and grain-size statistics.

  1. Inspect the field and extract atoms

    I inspect the real-space density field in ParaView. My C++/CUDA routines locate density maxima, turning the periodic field into discrete particle positions while retaining a density value for each peak.

  2. Compute orientations and write a dump

    For this monolayer workflow, I calculate in-plane orientation myself from sixfold bond order. I export the positions, density, orientation angle, and a quaternion representing rotation about the layer normal in a LAMMPS-style dump.

  3. Segment grains and calculate statistics

    My Python code selects grain cores using peak density and local distortion. Region growing joins periodic neighbors within a 3.5° misorientation tolerance, treating angles separated by 60° as equivalent. Neighborhood voting assigns boundary particles, followed by cleanup of small fragments and artifacts. Summing local atomic areas gives grain areas and equivalent diameters; CSV summaries then feed the grain-size distribution plots.

  4. Inspect particles and grains in OVITO

    I use OVITO to inspect particle positions and exported orientations and to visualize the segmented grains. I compare these views with the Python segmentation maps and grain-size distributions.

The definitions and protocol are given in Supplementary Section S3.3 of our preprint.
